Field notes · Strumica, North Macedonia

Every big fire starts as a small one nobody saw.

Phoenix is a drone-mounted smoke and fire detector, built by students. It looks for the first wisp, works out whether it is real, and puts an alert in your pocket.

Why we’re doing this

The gap between “it started” and “someone noticed.”

Summers around Strumicko are dry, and the hills burn. The fires that end up in the news almost always began as something tiny: a thin thread of smoke on a slope that nobody happened to be looking at.

That gap, between the first spark and the first person who sees it, is where a fire you can put out turns into one you can only watch. Closing it is mostly a question of seeing earlier, and seeing more of the land than a few people with binoculars ever could.

The tools that do this already exist: satellites, thermal cameras, networks of towers. They are also expensive, and built for governments, not for a village, a municipality or a school club. So we asked a different question. How far can you get with an ordinary camera, free software and a handful of cheap parts?

We are students. We don’t think that is a reason to wait for someone else to solve it.

How it works

From the first wisp to your phone.

  1. Step one

    Fly

    The drone follows its own route over the terrain. Our system rides along as a passenger; it does not control the flight.

  2. Step two

    Look

    A small camera streams what is below. No thermal sensor, just ordinary video, which is exactly what keeps the cost down.

  3. Step three

    Judge

    Software studies every frame for the colour, texture and movement of smoke and flame, and tries to tell them apart from things that only look like fire.

  4. Step four

    Tell

    A confirmed detection becomes a notification on a phone or laptop, so a person can decide what happens next.

Right now the judging step runs on a laptop. Moving it onto the drone comes later.
What we hold ourselves to

Four rules we won’t bend.

Cheap enough to copy.

If it needs a budget only a ministry has, it isn’t an answer for most places. Every part choice starts with the price.

Honest about what it is.

This is a student prototype. It will miss things and sometimes raise a false alarm. We would rather show that than hide it.

Alert, never act.

Phoenix tells people. Humans decide what to do about a fire, and no software should take that from them.

Open by default.

The detection code is being prepared for GitHub, so anyone can read it, improve it, or build their own version.
